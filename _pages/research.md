My research develops learning, optimization, and control methods for **large-scale interconnected cyber-physical systems**. The central goal is to make intelligent infrastructure adaptive enough to respond to uncertainty while remaining safe, feasible, interpretable, and useful to operators.

My Ph.D. dissertation, [*A Hierarchical Reinforcement Learning-Based Framework for Smart Cities Management*](https://scholarscompass.vcu.edu/etd/8343/), introduced a unified multi-level, multi-objective framework for coordinating smart buildings, intelligent transportation, and smart healthcare. The work combines hierarchical decision decomposition, sensitivity analysis, and adaptive resource allocation to improve coordination across domains without sacrificing their modularity.

## Research methodology

I follow an end-to-end workflow:

1. **Model the system:** translate physical constraints, uncertainty, and competing objectives into structured control and optimization problems.
2. **Develop the method:** integrate machine learning with robust optimization, model predictive control, or safe reinforcement learning.
3. **Evaluate rigorously:** compare against rule-based and optimization baselines under stress, uncertainty, and non-stationary conditions.
4. **Validate in hardware:** connect algorithms to sensors, embedded platforms, middleware, dashboards, and closed-loop experiments.

## Research thrusts

### Safe and scalable coordination for EV fast charging

Electrified transportation couples customer service decisions with feeder limits, voltage constraints, converter capabilities, renewable generation, and uncertain arrivals. I develop hierarchical coordination methods in which fast local controllers serve charging stations while a higher-level coordinator preserves network feasibility and fairness. Constraint-aware learning, action shielding, and feasibility projection keep decisions valid in real time.

### Resilient cyber-physical pipelines

Autonomous infrastructure depends on trustworthy sensing and data pipelines. This work connects data-quality monitoring, anomaly detection, probabilistic forecasting, and downstream control so models are assessed not only by prediction accuracy but also by their effect on safety, reliability, efficiency, and system recovery.

### Forecast-to-control optimization for microgrids

Microgrids and distributed-energy systems must anticipate uncertainty while respecting storage, converter, demand-response, and grid-interaction constraints. I co-design probabilistic forecasting with risk-sensitive optimization, model predictive control, and learning-based policies to improve cost, resilience, and operational reliability.

### Risk-aware learning for complex systems

This thrust develops the theoretical foundation that links calibrated uncertainty with robust and stochastic control. Confidence bounds inform risk-sensitive objectives, constraint tightening, verified fallback policies, and adaptive decisions under volatility, distribution shift, and partial observability.

## Collaboration and mentoring

I view research, education, and mentorship as inseparable. I have mentored more than 20 high-school, undergraduate, and graduate researchers from problem formulation through implementation and evaluation. My goal is to build collaborative teams where students develop strong foundations in modeling, algorithms, reproducible experimentation, technical writing, and research ethics while contributing to ambitious interdisciplinary work.
