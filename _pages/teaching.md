I aim to create a classroom that is **rigorous, supportive, and connected to real engineering systems**. Effective teaching is more than organized content delivery: it requires clear explanations, purposeful examples, active problem solving, and timely feedback that helps students become independent engineers.

## Teaching philosophy

Five principles guide my approach:

1. **Theory becomes durable through examples.** I connect mathematical ideas to physical systems before moving to formal derivations and implementation.
2. **Every lesson needs purpose and a roadmap.** Students should know what they are learning, why it matters, and how the pieces fit together.
3. **One learning path does not fit everyone.** I maintain high standards while using scaffolded practice, targeted feedback, and multiple forms of support.
4. **Questions are a strength.** A classroom where students can ask questions safely reveals misconceptions early and strengthens collective understanding.
5. **Availability and mentorship matter.** Office hours, prompt communication, and career or project guidance extend learning beyond class time.

I treat instruction as an iterative cycle—**plan, teach, assess, and refine**. Short concept checks, worked examples, and systematic debugging routines help me adjust pacing and support in response to student learning.

## Teaching experience

As a Teaching Assistant at Virginia Commonwealth University, I supported core undergraduate and graduate ECE courses across programming, digital systems, control, and embedded computing:

- **EGRE 335 — Signals and Systems:** continuous- and discrete-time systems, convolution, Fourier analysis, Laplace and Z transforms.
- **EGRE 364 — Microcomputer Systems:** Vivado workflows, simulation, hardware interfaces, and real-time applications.
- **EGRE 254 — Digital Logic Design:** five semesters of combinational/sequential design, discrete logic, and FPGA laboratories.
- **EGRE 245 — Engineering Programming:** five semesters of C programming, algorithms, structured problem solving, and software testing.
- **EGRE 553 — Industrial Automation:** PLCs, SCADA, and human-machine interfaces.
- **EGRE 365 — Digital Systems:** VHDL modeling, simulation, synthesis, FPGA design, and microprocessor interfacing.
- **EGRE 246 — Advanced Engineering Programming:** intermediate and advanced C programming for engineering applications.

These experiences showed me that students often struggle most when they cannot connect abstraction to physical meaning, encounter toolchain friction, or lack a systematic debugging strategy. I address these bottlenecks with stepwise verification—inputs, logic, outputs—and project-based exercises that make reasoning visible.

## Teaching interests

I am prepared to teach undergraduate courses in engineering programming, digital logic, embedded systems, computer organization, signals and systems, control, cyber-physical systems, and capstone design. At the graduate level, my interests include:

- Machine learning and artificial intelligence for engineering systems
- Feedback, digital, and model predictive control
- Smart-grid analytics and energy-system optimization
- Embedded and edge intelligence
- Safe and constrained reinforcement learning
- Resilient cyber-physical systems and anomaly detection

## Courses and modules I would develop

**AI for Smart Cities and Critical Infrastructure** would cover sensing-to-decision pipelines, time-series learning, safety-centered evaluation, and case studies across buildings, mobility, and energy.

**Safe and Trustworthy Learning for Cyber-Physical Systems** would connect constrained reinforcement learning, robustness, uncertainty-aware decision-making, and verification-oriented evaluation with hands-on implementation.

Across these courses, my goal is to help students move confidently from models and algorithms to implementation, verification, responsible deployment, and meaningful careers.
